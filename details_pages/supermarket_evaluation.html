Supermarket Evaluation

Overall Performance

  • Success Rate (SR): Cart-addition rate overall and by interaction turn.
    SR=100N∑e=1NCe
    SR(t)=100N∑e=1N1[Ce=1∧Te≤t]

    N is the number of episodes in the reported cohort or subset. Cₑ is one when the final cart is nonempty and zero otherwise; every episode contributes once.

    Tₑ counts distinct recorded interaction turns. SR(t) counts purchases completed by turn t, using the same full denominator at every point. Both rates are percentages.

  • Interaction Turns (T): Mean number of recorded interaction turns per episode.
    Te=|ℛe|
    T―=1N∑e=1NTe

    ℛₑ is the set of distinct turns with a saved interaction record. Multiple records from the same turn count once. The mean includes all episodes, including non-purchases.

    Cart addition ends a successful episode, so its total turn count is also its purchase turn. T is a count, not a percentage.

  • Termination Reason Share: Share of episodes ending for each recorded termination reason.
    Share(f)=100N∑e=1N1[terminal(e)=f]

    f is a recorded ending such as cart addition, the turn limit, recommendation exhaustion, a moderator stop, or a motion stall. Each distribution uses all tasks for that model and environment.

    Use the saved terminal reason; do not treat every non-purchase as a turn-limit failure.

  • Mean and Sample SD: Reported mean and variation across four profile-stratified subsets.
    μ―=14∑g=14μg
    s=13∑g=14(μg−μ―)2

    For GPT-5.6 Sol-user results reported with SDs, each environment’s 100 tasks form four disjoint subsets of 25, each covering all 25 profiles. Each profile’s four tasks are shuffled with seed 42, with one assigned to each subset; the partition is shared across assistants and ablations.

    μg is the eligible-episode mean in subset g, in the displayed units. s is the sample SD of the four subset means, not a confidence interval or the SD of all individual trajectories. GPT-4o-user results report cohort means without SDs; event-pooled diagnostics retain their stated event denominators.

Preference Alignment

  • Cart-R@5: Agreement between top-five recommendations and final-cart products.
    qet=|unique(Ret[1:5])∩Pe||Pe|
    se(q)={Me−1∑t=1Meqet,|Pe|Me>0,0,otherwise,
    Cart-R@5=100N∑e=1Nse(q)

    Pₑ is the set of distinct products in the final cart, Rₑₜ is the saved ranked list at turn t, and Mₑ is the number of saved recommendation lists. Repeated products receive credit only at their first occurrence without shifting later positions.

    Average lists within each episode, then average all episodes. Empty lists score zero; an empty cart or no recommendation records makes the entire episode score zero. Non-purchases remain in N. Supermarket matches normalized product names; Furnishings uses canonical identities and aliases from each run’s frozen full catalog.

  • Cart-NDCG@10: Top-ten ranking quality with final-cart products as relevance targets.
    zet=∑r=110reletr/log2(r+1)∑r=1min(10,|Pe|)1/log2(r+1)
    se(z)={Me−1∑t=1Mezet,|Pe|Me>0,0,otherwise,
    Cart-NDCG@10=100N∑e=1Nse(z)

    relₑₜᵣ is one only for the first occurrence of a final-cart product at rank r. The denominator is the ideal discounted gain for the number of distinct final-cart products, capped at ten.

    Average within each episode and then across all episodes. Empty lists, absent recommendation records and non-purchases score zero under the same rules as Cart-R@5. This measures agreement with realized choices, including purchased products outside the reference set.

  • Reference Purchase Rate (RPR): Share of all episodes purchasing products from the frozen reference set.
    Be=1[Pe≠∅∧Pe⊆Ae]
    RPR=100N∑e=1NBe

    Aₑ is the task’s frozen reference item set; Pₑ is the final-cart product set. A qualifying purchase has a nonempty cart whose products all belong to Aₑ. A non-purchase contributes zero.

    Reference membership uses required attributes, budget limits and user-specific avoidance constraints. Soft preferences are not inclusion criteria. Supermarket uses frozen product names; Furnishings uses canonical identities and aliases.

  • RPR | Cart: Reference-set purchase rate conditional on making a purchase.
    RPR∣Cart=100∑e=1NBe∑e=1NCe

    Bₑ is the reference-set purchase indicator defined for RPR; Cₑ indicates any cart addition. Only purchases enter the denominator, while non-purchases are excluded.

    The rate is undefined when there are no purchases. Different assistants can have different purchase subsets, so this conditional rate should be read together with SR and RPR.

  • R-MRR@10: Reciprocal rank of the first reference item in the top ten.
    uet={1/ret*,ret*≤10,0,otherwise,
    R-MRR@10=100|ER|∑e∈ER1Me∑t=1Meuet

    r*ₑₜ is the position of the first product belonging to the task-specific reference set Aₑ, or infinity when none occurs. A list without a top-ten reference item scores zero.

    Average saved ranking lists within an episode, then average eligible episodes Eᴿ with a defined reference-set ranking score. This supplementary metric uses the reference set, whereas Cart-R@5 and Cart-NDCG@10 use final-cart products.

  • Catalog Validity: Share of ranked entries found in the frozen product catalog.
    ve=∑i∈Ie1[i is catalog-valid]|Ie|
    Catalog=100|EI|∑e∈EIve

    Iₑ contains checkable ranked entries in episode e, and Eᴵ contains episodes with a nonzero entry denominator. Validity uses the saved frozen-catalog checks; entries without a usable product name are not checkable.

    The paper’s catalog column averages episode-level validity, using four-subset means and SDs for GPT-5.6 Sol users. Catalog membership does not imply reference-set membership, preference satisfaction or a successful purchase.

Guidance Effectiveness

  • Action Following Rate (AFR): Agreement between the intended and actual legal user action types.
    AFRe=∑t∈Ve1[a^et=aet]|Ve|
    AFR=100|EV|∑e∈EVAFRe

    Vₑ contains saved events whose expected tool is legal in the environment; Eⱽ contains episodes with nonempty Vₑ. The expected action is the structured expected user action, falling back to the first structured assistant action when necessary; the actual action is the user’s first tool call in that turn.

    Missing or different actual actions score zero. Missing or illegal expected tools are excluded. AFR checks tool-name agreement independently of arguments or execution success. Average eligible episodes within each reported subset or cohort.

  • Local Navigation Alignment (LNSR): Joint agreement on action type and comparable local parameters.
    LNSRe=∑t∈De1[a^et=aet]bet|De|
    LNSR=100|ED|∑e∈EDLNSRe

    Dₑ contains legal expected actions with comparable local parameters, bₑₜ indicates a parameter match, and Eᴰ contains episodes with nonempty Dₑ. A comparable expected action with a missing or mismatched actual tool or parameter scores zero; an unavailable expected direction or target is excluded.

    Five directional actions compare normalized direction, not angle magnitude, distance or duration. Supermarket additionally compares move-to-fixture target identity. Furnishings compares store/area targets and, for move-to-store, approach when supplied. Saved canonical matching resolves the roles’ different marker labels.

    The main-table definition requires tool and parameter matches together over Dₑ; it does not condition the denominator on the tool already matching.

  • Cumulative Goal Visibility (CGV): Share of eligible navigation goals visible by a given horizon.
    ug,s=1[visible(g,tg+s)]
    CGVh=100|G|∑g∈Gmax0≤s≤hug,s

    G pools navigation-goal events with a checkable current observation. u is one when a saved observation shows the goal. A goal counts once if visible now or in any saved observation through horizon h. All horizons use the same event denominator.

    Supermarket checks fixture/container observations; Furnishings checks recorded store/area/product relations. End extends through the saved episode end. Visibility comes from observations rather than the assistant’s own visibility claim.

  • Goal Visibility Gain (ΔCGV): Increase in cumulative visibility relative to the current observation.
    ΔCGVh=CGVh−CGV0
    h∈{1,2,3,4,5,End}

    The gain is measured in percentage points from the current-step baseline, not from the preceding horizon. Keep all checkable goal events in the denominator rather than restricting it to goals initially invisible.

  • Assistant Action Share: Distribution over the ten primary assistant dialogue actions.
    Share(a)=100|H|∑j∈H1[primary(j)=a]

    H contains all recorded assistant responses for one model and environment. Each response contributes one primary dialogue action; shares pool responses across all episodes.

  • Navigate Action Share: Distribution of suggested user actions within Navigate responses.
    NavShare(a)=100|Hnav|∑j∈Hnav1[a^j=a]

    Hₙₐᵥ contains all Navigate responses. The numerator counts suggestions of action type a; the denominator is all Navigate responses, as used for the bubble-area shares in the paper.

  • Navigate Same-Action Rate: Action-type following rate for each suggested Navigate action.
    Ha={j∈Hnav:a^j=a}
    Follow(a)=100|Ha|∑j∈Ha1[aj=a^j]

    The denominator contains suggestions of the corresponding action type, not all Navigate responses. Matching follows AFR’s action-name criterion; a missing or different actual action scores zero. Zero-use categories have no defined following rate.

  • Pearson r: Linear association between paired assistant-level mean scores.
    r=∑i=1n(xi−x―)(yi−y―)∑i=1n(xi−x―)2∑i=1n(yi−y―)2

    xᵢ and yᵢ are paired means for the same assistant, comparing human with automatic-user results or questionnaire with judge ratings. The paper uses five paired assistant means, not individual trajectories, as the correlation units.

    Use unrounded means. Pearson r is undefined if either variable has no variation; these comparisons are descriptive.

  • Spearman ρ: Rank agreement between paired assistant-level mean scores.
    ρ=corr(rank(x),rank(y))

    Apply Pearson correlation to average ranks, assigning midranks to ties. The paper compares five paired assistant-level means using unrounded values, treating differences below numerical precision as ties.

  • Kendall τb: Pairwise ranking agreement with correction for ties.
    τb=C−D(C+D+Tx)(C+D+Ty)

    C and D count concordant and discordant assistant pairs. Tₓ and Tᵧ count pairs tied only in the corresponding variable; pairs tied in both are excluded. The paper uses five paired assistant means, not trajectories.

    Use unrounded means and the same numerical tie handling as the other model-level comparisons. A zero denominator is undefined.
